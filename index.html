The plasma promo pays $70 an hour exactly once

Plasma centers lead with the new-donor month: about $700 across your first eight donations. Twice a week is the cadence, and each visit runs about 75 minutes from the screening chair to the juice and the bandage. Eight visits at 75 minutes is 10 hours of your month. The $700 across those 10 hours is the $70 an hour the ads are quoting.

Month two is the real rate. The promo lapses to a base of about $30 a visit, and the chair time does not change. Eight visits, 10 hours, $240. The hourly fell from $70 to $24, a two-thirds pay cut for identical work, and nothing about the schedule or the needle changed with it.

The car takes its cut either way. A 12-mile round trip eight times a month is 96 miles, and at the 70 cents a mile the IRS prices a car at, driving to be paid costs $67.20. The base month nets $172.80 across its 10 hours, which is $17.28 an hour. The promo month nets $632.80, or $63.28 an hour. Same miles, and the sticker month hides them behind the bonus.

The deferral is the wage risk. A protein or iron fail ends the visit at the screening chair, unpaid, about 20 minutes in. Two deferrals in a base month leave six paid visits: $180 across just under 8.2 hours including the two unpaid screening stops, which is $22.03 an hour and $60 gone from the month. No warehouse dock deducts your pay for showing up dehydrated.

Month two tells the truth.
